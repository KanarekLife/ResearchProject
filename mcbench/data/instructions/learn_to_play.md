# Marvel Champions: The Card Game — learn to play (solo)

A condensed paraphrase of the official Learn to Play book, in its order, limited to a solo game (one player, one hero). Deck building, the starter deck lists and the scenario lists are left out.

## Overview
You play one Marvel hero, both as the hero and as their alter-ego. You try to defeat a villain, controlled by the game, who is trying to complete an evil scheme.

## Key concepts
- **Golden rule:** if a card's text directly contradicts the rules, the card's text wins.
- **Ready and exhausted:** cards enter play ready (upright). Some abilities require a card to exhaust (turn sideways); an exhausted card cannot exhaust again until it is readied.
- **Per player:** a value with the per-player icon is multiplied by the number of players (1 in solo).
- **Winning and losing:** the villain is a sequence of numbered stages. Reducing a stage's hit points to zero defeats it and advances to the next stage; defeating the final stage wins the game. The villain wins if it completes its scheme (the threat on the final card of its scheme deck reaches the amount that card requires) or if your hit points are reduced to zero.

## Setup
1. Choose your identity card and place it alter-ego side up.
2. Set your hit point dial to the hit points printed on the identity card.
3. Set aside your obligation card.
4. Set aside your nemesis and the encounter cards that go with it. They are not in play, but card abilities may bring them in during the game.
5. Shuffle your player deck.
6. Gather the damage tokens, threat tokens and counters, and the stunned, confused and tough status cards.
7. Put the villain deck (stages in order, stage I on top) and the main scheme deck into play.
8. Set the villain's hit point dial to the value on its card (times the number of players).
9. Resolve any "Setup" instructions on the main scheme, then turn it to its next side.
10. Shuffle your obligation into the villain's encounter deck. Your nemesis cards stay set aside until an encounter card calls for them.
11. Draw cards up to your hand size (printed near the bottom left of the identity card).
12. Mulligan: discard any cards you do not want from your hand, then draw back up to your hand size. The discarded cards are not shuffled back into your deck now.
13. Resolve any "Setup" instructions on your identity.

## Round sequence
The game is played in rounds. Each round has a player phase and then a villain phase, and the game alternates between them until you win or the villain wins.

## Player phase
During your turn you may do the following, in any order. Each may be done as many times as you have the cards and resources to pay for it, except changing form.
- Change form from hero to alter-ego or from alter-ego to hero. **Only once each turn.**
- Play an ally, upgrade or support card from your hand.
- Use your alter-ego's basic recovery (in alter-ego form), or your hero's basic attack or basic thwart (in hero form).
- Use an ally you control to attack an enemy or thwart a scheme.
- Trigger an "Action" ability on a card you control in play, on an encounter card in play, or by playing an event with such an ability from your hand. An ability marked "Hero Action" or "Alter-Ego Action" needs you to be in that form.

### Changing form
Flip your identity card to its other side. Your character stays in the same state (ready or exhausted), and keeps its upgrades, attachments, tokens, damage and status cards.

### Playing a card
1. Place the card on the table.
2. Pay its cost.
3. An ally, upgrade or support enters play ready in your play area. An event resolves and then goes to your discard pile.

### Resource costs
- You generate resources in two ways: discard a card from your hand to generate one resource per resource icon on it (bottom-left corner), or use a card with a "Resource" ability.
- Resources generated beyond the cost are lost after paying.
- The resource types are mental, physical, energy and wild. A wild resource can be used as any other type.
- Usually any type, or mix of types, can pay a card's cost, but many abilities and encounter effects require specific types.

### Ally limit
You may have up to three allies in play. If you control three, you may still play a different ally, but you immediately discard one of your previous allies.

### Using a basic power
Basic powers are on your identity card: the hero side has basic attack, basic thwart and basic defense; the alter-ego side has basic recovery. You must be in the form that has the power. To use one, declare it, pay its cost by exhausting your identity, and resolve it:
- **Basic recovery:** heal hit points equal to your REC. You cannot heal above your maximum hit points.
- **Basic thwart:** remove threat equal to your THW from one scheme of your choice. Any scheme can be thwarted unless a card ability (such as the crisis icon) prevents it.
- **Basic attack:** deal damage equal to your ATK to one enemy. Any enemy, villain or minion, can be attacked unless a card ability (such as the guard keyword) prevents it.
- **Basic defense:** used only when you are attacked. Exhaust your hero to prevent damage equal to your DEF; you take the damage from the attack that exceeds your DEF.

### Dealing damage
- Damage on a minion or ally is placed on the card as damage tokens. A minion or ally with zero hit points remaining is defeated and goes to its owner's discard pile.
- Damage on you or the villain lowers the hit point dial. When the villain's dial reaches zero, that stage is defeated and the next stage is revealed with the hit points shown on it. Defeating the final stage wins. When your dial reaches zero, you are eliminated.

### Using an ally
1. Exhaust the ally (an exhausted ally cannot be used).
2. Declare whether it attacks an enemy or thwarts a scheme.
3. Declare the target.
4. Deal its ATK as damage to the enemy, or remove its THW in threat from the scheme.
5. Consequential damage: deal the ally one damage for each damage icon under the ATK or THW it used.

### Triggering an action ability
1. Declare the ability.
2. Pay its cost, which comes before the arrow (→). Generated resources must match the resources shown. Some abilities have no cost; some have extra costs such as "exhaust this card".
3. Resolve its effect.

An action ability may be triggered any number of times per turn, as long as you can pay its costs and its effect can change the game.

### Other triggered abilities
- **Interrupt:** used when its condition is about to happen, just before it does. It can prevent the occurrence or change it into something else.
- **Response:** used immediately after its condition has happened.
- **Resource:** used whenever you are generating resources to pay a cost.
- These abilities are optional unless they say "Forced". Each copy can be used once each time its condition occurs.

### Hero and alter-ego abilities
An ability marked "Hero" or "Alter-Ego", or whose text refers to a form, can be used only in that form. An ability that names no form works in either form.

### Action abilities on encounter cards
Some encounter cards have action abilities you may use during your turn while they are in play, as often as you pay their cost. (For example, an attachment on the villain may let you pay resources in hero form to discard it.)

### Ending your turn
When you have done everything you can or want to, your turn ends. Then:
1. You may discard any number of cards from your hand, and must discard down to your hand size if you have more.
2. Draw cards until your hand equals your hand size.
3. Ready all of your cards.

## Villain phase
The villain activates, minions attack, and new encounter cards come into play.

### The scheme deck
- The main scheme deck is the villain's goal. If the villain completes the final stage, it wins.
- Each main scheme has a target threat. Threat is checked all the time: as soon as the threat on it equals or exceeds the target, the scheme deck advances to the next stage and the old threat is removed. If the final stage advances, you lose immediately.
- Some main schemes start with threat on them.
- When an enemy schemes, threat equal to its SCH is placed on the main scheme.

### Step one: place threat
Place the threat shown in the main scheme's acceleration field on it, plus 1 for each side scheme in play with an acceleration icon.

### Step two: villain and minion activations
The villain activates against you, then each minion engaged with you activates. In **alter-ego** form they scheme; in **hero** form they attack.

**Alter-ego form: enemies scheme.**
1. Give the villain a face-down card from the encounter deck as its boost card.
2. Turn it face up. Each boost icon in its bottom-right corner gives the villain +1 SCH for this activation. A star icon gives no SCH; it means the card's text has a matching ability, marked with a star, that resolves now. Discard the boost card.
3. Place threat on the main scheme equal to the villain's modified SCH.
4. Each engaged minion then schemes, one at a time, placing its SCH as threat. Minions get no boost cards.

**Hero form: enemies attack.**
1. Give the villain a face-down boost card.
2. Choose whether to defend, by exhausting your hero or one of your allies.
3. Turn the boost card face up. Each boost icon gives +1 ATK; a star means its starred ability resolves. Discard it.
4. Deal damage equal to the villain's modified ATK: if your hero defends, it is reduced by your DEF and the rest is dealt to your hero; if an ally defends, all of it is dealt to the ally; if no one defends, all of it is dealt to your hero.
5. Each engaged minion then attacks, one at a time, the same way but using only its ATK. Minions get no boost cards.

**Strategy tip (from the book).** Your form decides what the villain does each round. To stop the villain scheming, be in hero form to confront it. If you are so worn down that an attack could be dangerous, it may be better to stay in alter-ego form to recover and build up your forces; but in alter-ego form you are not stopping the villain's plans. Balancing your hero and alter-ego is one of the key skills of the game.

### Step three: deal encounter cards
You are dealt one face-down card from the encounter deck, plus one for each side scheme in play with a hazard icon.

### Step four: reveal encounter cards
Reveal and resolve the dealt cards one at a time. Each card resolves by type:
- **Minion:** enters play engaged with you.
- **Treachery:** resolve its effect, then put it in the encounter discard pile.
- **Attachment:** enters play attached to the villain.
- **Side scheme:** enters play next to the main scheme with threat already on it. Threat can be removed from it by basic thwarts and card abilities; when it has no threat, it is discarded.
- **Obligation:** it is yours; follow its instructions to resolve it.

Side scheme icons:
- **Crisis:** must be defeated before threat can be removed from the main scheme.
- **Hazard:** one more encounter card is dealt in step three.
- **Acceleration:** one more threat is placed on the main scheme in step one.

Also:
- **When Revealed** abilities resolve right after the card enters play (for a treachery, before it is discarded).
- **When Defeated** abilities resolve when you defeat the minion, villain stage or scheme that has them.
- An encounter ability marked "Hero" or "Alter-Ego" resolves only if you are in that form.
- **Nemesis side schemes:** when a card puts your nemesis side scheme into play, your nemesis minion also enters play engaged with you, and the other cards of your nemesis set go to the encounter discard pile.
- **Surge:** after resolving a card with surge, reveal one more encounter card.

### Step five: end the round
The round ends and the next player phase begins.

## Status cards
- **Stunned:** if a stunned hero or ally tries to attack or use an attack ability, discard the stun instead; if the attempt would exhaust it, it still exhausts. If a stunned villain would attack, discard the stun instead.
- **Confused:** if a confused hero or ally tries to thwart or use a thwart ability, discard the confuse instead; if the attempt would exhaust it, it still exhausts. If a confused villain would scheme, discard the confuse instead.
- **Tough:** if a character with a tough card would take any damage, prevent all of it and discard the tough card instead.

## Abbreviations and icons
- ATK attack, DEF defense, REC recover, SCH scheme, THW thwart.
- The arrow (→) separates a cost from an effect.
- The unique icon before a title means the card is unique.
