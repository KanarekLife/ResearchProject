# How to play Marvel Champions solo: a community tutorial

This is how experienced players usually teach the game, followed by the advice they give most often.

## The game in one paragraph
You play one Marvel hero against a villain controlled by the game. The villain is trying to finish a scheme: threat builds up on the main scheme card, and if it reaches the number printed on the card, you lose. You win by knocking out the villain, who has more than one stage: defeating one stage brings out the next at full health. You also lose if your own hit points reach zero.

## Setting up
- Your identity card starts with the alter-ego side up; set your hit points to the number on the card.
- Your obligation is shuffled into the encounter deck. Your nemesis cards are set aside; they only appear if an encounter card calls for them.
- The villain, its main scheme and the encounter deck are set up, and the main scheme's setup instructions are resolved.
- Draw up to your hand size. Once, you may throw away any of those cards and draw back up (the mulligan).

## Your turn
Do any of these in any order:
- **Change form** between hero and alter-ego, once per turn. Some cards can only be played in one form, so plan the order of your plays around your one flip.
- **Play cards** by paying their cost (top left) with the resource icons (bottom left) of other cards you discard from your hand. Most cards give one resource; a few give two. Resources from an ability must be spent right away; they cannot be saved.
- **Use a basic power**, which exhausts your identity: attack (ATK) or thwart (THW) as a hero, recover (REC) as an alter-ego.
- **Use allies** to attack or thwart. They exhaust and then take consequential damage, the small damage icons under their ATK or THW. Many allies take 1, some more.
- **Use actions** on your cards in play.

At the end of your turn, discard any cards you want, draw up to your hand size (each form has its own hand size, usually smaller for the hero) and ready everything. Your cards ready only now: a hero or ally that defends during the villain phase stays exhausted and cannot attack or thwart in your next turn.

## The villain's turn
1. Threat goes on the main scheme: the amount printed on it, plus 1 for each acceleration icon or token.
2. The villain activates, then each minion engaged with you. If you are a **hero**, they attack you. If you are an **alter-ego**, they scheme and add threat equal to their SCH. A scheme cannot be defended.
3. Every time the villain attacks or schemes, it gets a face-down **boost card** from the encounter deck. Its boost icons add to the attack or scheme. If it has a star, its "Boost:" text also happens. Any other text on the boost card is ignored.
4. When you are attacked you can defend with your hero (exhaust; your DEF reduces the damage), defend with an ally (exhaust; the ally takes all the damage), or take it.
5. You are dealt one encounter card, plus one for each hazard icon, and reveal it. Minions engage you, treacheries happen and are discarded, side schemes come into play with threat on them, attachments attach to the villain, and your obligation comes to you.

Side scheme icons: **crisis** stops you removing threat from the main scheme, **acceleration** adds 1 threat every villain phase, **hazard** adds one encounter card every villain phase.

## Rules new players often miss
- Allies take consequential damage every time they attack or thwart.
- A defending ally that takes more damage than its hit points is defeated, and the extra damage is lost. Only an attack with overkill carries the extra to you.
- A tough status stops all the damage of the next hit.
- When your deck runs out and you reshuffle your discard pile, you are dealt an extra encounter card.
- A villain moving to its next stage with the same name keeps its status cards and attachments. Damage beyond the stage's hit points never carries over.
- Whether an effect is an attack or a thwart matters for what can stop it.
- Do every step of the villain phase: threat, boost cards, side scheme effects, crisis.
- If you keep losing, check that you are not playing a rule in the villain's favour by mistake.

## Advice players give most often
**Control the board, then go for the villain.**
- Deal with side schemes and minions first, keep the main scheme low, and hit the villain last. Damage on the villain changes nothing until the stage is defeated, while every minion and side scheme keeps doing its full effect.
- Finish what you start. A minion with 1 hit point left or a side scheme with 1 threat left is as dangerous as a fresh one. Defeating a 2-hit-point minion is better than putting 2 damage on the villain.
- Kill guard minions at once, since they stop you attacking the villain. Other minions can sometimes be left: absorb their attack, thwart instead, or stun or confuse them.
- Know when to stop controlling and push. Once you can defeat the villain, nothing else matters; leftover threat and minions do not count.

**Stay in hero form in solo.**
- In alter-ego form the villain and its minions scheme, and one big boost can add a lot of threat at once. Flip to alter-ego only when the main scheme can take it, and keep a fallback.
- Going alter-ego to recover is best when the board is clear.
- Flipping regularly is worth it when both sides of your identity have useful abilities, and the alter-ego's larger hand size draws you more cards.

**Use your whole hand.**
- You draw back up at the end of every turn, so cards left in hand are wasted tempo. Try to end your turn with an empty hand.
- Do not be afraid to pay with a good card. It goes to your discard pile and comes back when your deck reshuffles.
- Value still matters: a card that keeps working is worth more early than late, and several cheap plays can beat one expensive one.

**Use your basic power every turn.** Your identity readies before the next turn anyway. Early in the game, with little threat to remove, attack.

**Build a board early.**
- Mulligan for upgrades, supports and allies that keep working every round, even if that means discarding decent events.
- Against a fast villain, do not spend too many turns setting up.

**Allies are shields.**
- An ally's best use is often: arrive, use its ability, attack or thwart, then spend its last hit points blocking a villain attack. A blocked attack is usually worth more than one more thwart.
- Do not chump-block an attack with overkill; the extra damage reaches you.
- Keep allies with useful ongoing abilities in play instead of throwing them in front of attacks.
- Defending with your hero keeps you in hero form, but leaves your hero exhausted for your next turn.

**Plan for the villain phase.**
- Before ending your turn, count the possible damage (villain plus boost plus every minion) and the possible threat.
- Use the stun and confuse statuses to skip a dangerous attack or scheme.
- Keep track of which dangerous encounter cards have already appeared (the encounter discard pile is public) and which may still come.

**Threat past the limit.** If the main scheme has further stages, threat beyond its threshold is lost when it advances, so do not spend resources on a scheme that will advance anyway. Remember that the next stage starts with its own threat and effects. If the current stage is the last one, reaching the threshold loses the game.
