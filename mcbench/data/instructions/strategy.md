# Strategy guide (solo)

## The core tension
You lose to either of two clocks: your hit points or the main scheme's threat. The villain only beats you on one of them at a time. As a hero you are attacked and threat grows slowly; as an alter-ego you are safe from attacks, but the villain and minions scheme. Most turns come down to which clock you can afford to let run.

## Play fast and aggressively
Games are won by pressure. Every round the villain survives brings another villain phase, more threat and at least one more encounter card, and each reshuffle of the encounter deck adds an acceleration token. Waiting makes the game harder, not safer.
- **Early game.** The first rounds are the calmest of the game: threat is low and the villain is on its first stage. Use them to build and to attack at the same time: get allies, supports and upgrades into play, use them every turn, and damage the villain from your first turn as a hero.
- **Attack by default.** Spend your hero's basic power on an attack whenever the coming villain phase leaves the main scheme safe. Remove threat with allies, supports and events, and thwart with your hero only when the scheme is in danger.
- **Damage is also threat control.** The faster each villain stage falls, the fewer villain phases you have to survive.
- **React only to real danger:** threat the next villain phase could complete, or damage that could defeat you (see the next section). Otherwise keep attacking.

## Plan every turn with the villain phase in mind
Before ending your turn, estimate what the coming villain phase will do:
- **Threat:** per-round threat + acceleration icons + acceleration tokens. In alter-ego form, add the villain's SCH, each engaged minion's SCH and the villain's boost card. Leave a margin for encounter cards that add threat.
- **Damage (hero form):** the villain's ATK plus attachment bonuses and its boost card, plus each engaged minion's ATK. Subtract what your defense will stop.
- Treat the boost card as unknown and plan for a high one: boost cards often carry 2 or 3 icons. Also allow for an encounter card that adds threat or makes an extra attack. Do not end a turn where that would make you lose.

If the estimated threat would complete the main scheme, removing threat comes before anything else. If the estimated damage would defeat you, defending, healing or changing to alter-ego comes first.

## Look for a win first
At the start of each turn, check whether the damage you can deal this turn (basic attack, allies, events, actions) is enough to defeat the villain's final stage. If it is, spend everything on that.

## Hero or alter-ego
- Stay in hero form while you can take the hits: it stops the villain scheming and lets you attack and thwart.
- Change to alter-ego when an attack could defeat you, when you need to recover, or when your alter-ego has abilities or cards that build your board. Leave it again before threat gets close to the target.
- You can change form once per turn, in either direction, at any point in the turn. A common pattern is to act as a hero (attack or thwart), then flip to alter-ego at the end of the turn to avoid the attack, or to recover as an alter-ego and then flip to hero so that the villain attacks instead of scheming. Changing form does not ready your identity: if it defended in the last villain phase, it stays exhausted in either form until the end of your turn.
- Ending a turn in alter-ego puts the villain's SCH, its boost and every minion's SCH on the main scheme at once. Add them up with a high boost before you change form, and stay a hero if the total could reach the target.
- A stun on the villain is wasted if you then end the turn in alter-ego, because the villain schemes instead of attacking.

## Use everything, every turn
- Your identity readies at the end of each player phase, before the villain phase, so using a basic power on your turn never stops you from defending. An unused basic power is wasted: attack, thwart or recover every turn you can.
- The same goes for each ready ally and each card in play with an action: one you did not use this turn is lost, not saved.
- Keep a playable card in hand only if you know when you will play it.

## Threat management
- Keep the main scheme well below its target. Threat can arrive in bursts from treacheries, side schemes and the villain's scheme.
- Side schemes with **acceleration** add threat every round and those with **hazard** draw extra encounter cards every round; remove them early. A **crisis** side scheme stops you from thwarting the main scheme, so it must be removed before the main scheme gets dangerous.
- Prefer thwarting a scheme that you can clear completely: a side scheme left with 1 threat still has all of its effects.

## Damage and the villain
- Damage beyond a villain stage's remaining hit points is lost. Finish a stage with the smallest effect that is enough, and save big attacks for a fresh stage.
- A tough status prevents a whole attack. Remove it with a small hit before using a big one.
- A stunned villain's next attack is replaced; a confused villain's next scheme is replaced. These are worth most just before the villain phase where that activation would hurt.

## Minions
- Minions add to both clocks every round. Defeat them when you can, especially those with **guard** (you cannot attack the villain), **patrol** (you cannot thwart the main scheme), high ATK, or high SCH.
- Match the damage to the minion: use the smallest effect that defeats it, and avoid wasting a large attack on a 1-hit-point minion.

## Allies
- Allies add attacks, thwarts and a body that can absorb an attack. Count consequential damage: an ally that will be defeated by its own consequential damage gets one last use.
- An ally that is about to be discarded anyway (at low hit points, or to make room under the ally limit) is a good defender.
- An ally that defends cannot attack or thwart on your next turn, and neither can your hero if it defends. Defend with whichever of the two has the less valuable next action.

## Defending
- A defender stays exhausted through your whole next turn, because you ready only at the end of the player phase. Defending with your hero trades next turn's basic power for the damage DEF stops: do it when that damage matters more, for example when the attack could defeat you or leave you too low to stay in hero form.
- Defend with an ally when the attack is large; the ally takes all of it, and the excess is lost unless the attack has overkill.
- Take an attack undefended only when you have the hit points to spare and your defenders are worth more ready.

## Cards and resources
- Every card is either a card to play or the payment for one. Decide which cards matter this turn and pay with the rest.
- Count resources before you start: playing a cheap card first can leave you unable to pay for the important one.
- Watch for cards whose effect depends on the resource type you paid with, and pay with that type.
- Permanent cards (allies, supports, upgrades) keep paying back every round. Play them early, when the board is calm.
- Events are one-off effects. Hold an event that responds to something (an interrupt or response) until its moment comes; hold an attack event for when it defeats a minion or finishes the villain.
- At the end of your turn you may discard cards you cannot use soon to draw new ones, but each time your deck runs out you are dealt an extra encounter card.

## Mulligan
Keep cards you can play in the first rounds, especially allies, upgrades and supports that build your board, plus enough resources to pay for them. Discard expensive cards and situational cards you will not need early.

## The encounter deck
- Each villain phase deals you at least one encounter card. Expect new minions, side schemes and treacheries, and leave room in your plan for them.
- When the encounter deck runs out, it is reshuffled and an acceleration token is added for the rest of the game.
- The encounter discard pile is open information. Checking what has already appeared tells you what is still in the deck.

## Common mistakes
- Ending the turn without checking the threat and damage that the villain phase will bring.
- Playing passively: ending turns with ready allies, unused actions or cards you could have played, and letting the villain live for extra rounds.
- Staying in alter-ego too long while threat piles up, or ending a turn in alter-ego when the villain's scheme with a high boost could complete the main scheme.
- Defending with your hero against an attack you could afford to take, and so losing next turn's basic power.
- Leaving a guard or patrol minion in play.
- Overkilling a villain stage or a weak minion with a big attack.
- Paying for a card with the card you needed next.
- Forgetting that a confused or stunned hero still exhausts when attempting the blocked action.
